Heavy rainstorms, hurricanes, tropical storms and flash floods can do serious water damage to homes and buildings in Vallejo. Wind-driven rain pushes water in through damaged roofing, broken windows and loose siding. A roof leak can soak insulation, ceilings and the cavities inside your walls. Flash flooding overwhelms drainage systems and pushes water into lower levels. After storm damage, the Vallejo Restoration team gets to work limiting further intrusion, removing standing water and starting the restoration process without delay.

Storm damage often causes several problems at once: a damaged roof lets water in, the water ruins ceilings and walls, and mold follows if nothing is dried properly. Our approach ties every one of those linked problems into a single, coordinated plan.

Storm Damage Repair Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Cracked caulk lets blown rain in, so inspect and renew the seals on doors and windows
  • Make sure the attic is properly ventilated and insulated to prevent ice dams
  • Take dated photos of the house every year, since insurers ask about prior condition
  • Before storm season each year, get the roof inspected and its problem areas repaired
  • Homes in hurricane country benefit from shutters or impact-rated window glass
  • Runoff should flow away from your foundation, so fix low spots and add downspout extensions

How to Tell You Need Storm Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Downspouts knocked off and gutter runs hanging after high wind
  • A musty smell indoors in the days after heavy rain
  • Water stains that show up on ceilings or walls during or after a storm
  • Swollen ceiling panels or blistered, peeling paint in the days after a storm
  • Flooding in lower levels because drainage systems were overwhelmed
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Rain blown in around door frames, window frames or openings in the wall
