Bathroom Water Damage Help in Vallejo

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. Our Vallejo team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

What It Looks Like

  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Leaking Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

Trapped Steam

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Slow Vanity Drips

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.
