Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Vallejo Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Vallejo, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
