Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Vallejo Homeowners Should Know

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

What It Looks Like

  • Door casing and trim joints separating from the drywall
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.
